# Architecture

EH3D-PHOENIX-3DP is a Cargo workspace: eight small libraries, none of which uses a crate from crates.io (the server bridge depends by path on the common client of the suite, `eh3d-serverclient` of the sibling repository EH3D-COMMON, which has no dependency either); the program itself also takes by path the panel of the settings of the server of `eh3d-view` of that repository, and through it what that crate uses, and the program that uses them (a library `eh3d_phoenix_3dp`, the command line `phoenix-3dp` and, with `--features gui`, a window). Every crate has `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]`. The picture of the README is drawn from `architecture.json`; this page says the same in words.

## Rules that hold across the crates

* **A limit is a refusal.** `TempLimits::check` returns an error for a value above the limit; nothing in the program lowers a value to the limit. The same function is used by the G-code writer, by the drivers (through `driver::guard`) and by the checker of programs.
* **Units.** Lengths are whole nanometres inside the program, so adding and comparing never loses a digit; a number in millimetres is turned into `Nm` once, with a check that it is finite and not beyond a kilometre. Text is written back with the exact decimals.
* **Sizes before allocation.** Every reader checks the size it is promised (the triangles of an STL header, the entries of a ZIP package, the size it inflates to) against `Limits` before it reserves memory.
* **Doors.** Raw G-code is a command (`Command::Gcode`) that is refused unless the setting is on, is one line, and goes through the checker first. A file name or an identifier that goes into a path is checked (`safe_file_name`, `path_id`) and percent-encoded.
* **Secrets.** The token and the keys are `Secret` values; `Debug` and `Display` print `***`, and the settings are shown with `to_text(false)`.
* **A network only where an address is given.** The drivers speak to an `HttpTransport`. The tests give them `SimMoonraker` or `SimOctoPrint` (a printer in memory that can fail on demand) or `TcpTransport` against a socket of `127.0.0.1` that the test itself opens; the server client is given `SimPrinterServer`, built on the server in memory of the suite. Nothing opens a connection unless an address is set; there is no TLS (an `https://` address is refused with the reason) and a redirect is not followed.

## The slicer

`p3dp-slicer` cuts the mesh at the middle of every layer (a vertex on the plane counts as above it, so the cuts of neighbouring triangles meet exactly), joins the segments into loops, offsets the loops inwards for the perimeters (cutting apart the loops that an offset folds over itself, and leaving out what is thinner than the perimeters, with a warning or a refusal as `thin_regions` says), fills the rest with straight lines, and makes the infill solid where the layers above or below do not cover it. Every value is in the settings file (`SliceConfig`, with a range for each key); a temperature over the limit of the model, a model that does not fit the bed, a mesh that is not closed or inside out and a setting out of its range are errors, never adjusted. The G-code is written through the `Writer` of the program, which refuses a coordinate outside the bed, so what the plan says and what the file says cannot differ.

### What the slicer lays on a layer

The sparse infill is rectilinear (lines that turn by a right angle on each layer), grid (both ways on every layer) or gyroid (the zero set of `sin x cos y + sin y cos z + sin z cos x`, followed on a grid of squares and joined from the segments it leaves in each of them; the period comes from the density). Every pattern is made of polylines cut in short pieces, each judged by its middle: solid where the layers above or below do not cover it, a bridge where the skin hangs over nothing (laid across the gap, in the direction that has more length held at both ends, printed slower with its own flow and fan, and refused when longer than `max_bridge_span_mm`), sparse infill elsewhere. The supports are decided on columns of the part sampled on a grid: a column whose material starts over empty space and has no lower material inside the cone of `support_overhang_angle_deg` gets crossed lines under it, with a gap at the top and a clearance at the sides; an overhang that would stand on the part while the supports are limited to the bed is refused. The seam of a perimeter is at the nearest corner, at the rear or lined up with the layer below. The perimeters of a part too thin for them may be made narrower (`width_variation_percent`) before the part is called thin. A travel that retracts may lift the head (`z_hop_mm`, refused when it does not fit the printer). The time is estimated by `timing`: each move is a block with a top speed, the speed at each joint is limited by the angle and by the acceleration over the length of the move (a pass back and a pass forward), and each block is a trapezoid; the plain estimate with no acceleration is kept beside it as the least a print can take.

## The normalized state

Every driver answers `PrinterState`: the family, one of fifteen states (`offline`, `idle`, `preparing`, `printing`, `pausing`, `paused`, `finishing`, `finished`, `failed`, `cancelling`, `cancelled`, `error`, `emergency`, `updating`, `busy`), the raw text of the printer, the temperatures of the heaters, the job (file, progress, time) and the alerts. The tables that map what each protocol says onto the fifteen states are in `p3dp-link/src/state_map.rs` and are tested one row at a time.

## The window

`app_state::AppState` holds everything the four tabs show (Prepare, Preview, Device, Project): the printer chosen, the model loaded, the reports in the chosen language, the camera, and a simulated device for the Device tab. `gui.rs` only draws it and passes on what the person does, so the logic is tested without a screen. The window is laid out like a slicer: a bar on top with the tabs, a panel at the left (printer, filament, process), the 3D view in the middle, a panel of the parts at the right and a bar at the bottom with the slice, its progress and the estimates. What the Prepare tab does is in modules without a window: `objects` keeps each part (position, turn, scale, size in millimetres, auto-orient among six stands, arrange in rows with a gap, overlaps, past the edge, joined into one mesh for the slicer), `shade` turns the parts into flat shaded polygons farthest first and draws the bed (grid, edge, box of the printer, axes) and the standard views, `studio` holds the camera, the warnings and the estimates and runs the slice on its own thread (`slice_job`, with a bar that is an estimate from the last slice and never reads full before the end), and `limits_ui` checks and keeps the temperature limits a person types. The look (`dark` or `light`), the grid, the gap and the hints are settings of the file. It is built with `--features gui` (egui) and has not been looked at on a screen yet. The viewer of its own is the crate `p3dp-viewer`: everything that decides what is seen (the camera, the picking, the range of layers, the lines and their colours) is plain arithmetic and is tested; the drawing is a pipeline of lines in wgpu behind the feature `gui` of that crate, never built.
