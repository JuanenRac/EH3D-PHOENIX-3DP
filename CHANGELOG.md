# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here.

## [Unreleased]
- Fix the offset of a loop inwards: past the middle of a shape (a pin thinner than the perimeters, a square inset by more than half its side) the corners crossed over and a loop came out the same way round, a shape that is not there, so a perimeter was laid where there is no room; such a loop is now dropped.
- Fix the profiles of printers, filaments and resins (security): a vendor, model or name with a line break or another control character could begin another line of the profile file it is written to (a key of its own, even `verified`), and one with ` #` was cut short when read back; both are now refused when the profile is checked.
- Fix the figures of a print: a value written with a blank before it (` 0.2`) was dropped instead of read.
- Fix the reading of a text STL whose name, or of an OBJ whose comment, is not UTF-8 (Latin-1 from some programs of Windows): the whole file was refused for a name that does not count; only the numbers are read.
- Fix the reading of a binary STL whose 80-byte header starts like the text of an OBJ (a `#`, `v ` or `o `): it was taken for an OBJ and refused; the size of the file now decides first.
- Add tests that hunt for bugs in the formats, the profiles and the slicer: the checksum and the inflate, the ZIP package, the XML reader, STL, OBJ and 3MF, the pictures, the thumbnails and the header of binary G-code, the catalogs and files of printers, filaments and resins, what a printer says about several materials, the geometry of a layer, the time of a run of moves, every range and rule of the settings and the refusals of a plan.
- **Behaviour tests at the exact edge of the core, the G-code and the link:** three new test files add 153 deterministic tests (the JSON and settings readers at their size and depth limits, the limits of the readers key by key, the lengths in nanometres, the mesh checks and their warnings, the temperature limits, the exact lines of each dialect for the temperatures, the fan, the moves, the start and the end of a job, the checker of programs, the tables of states of the four printer families, the pauses and the tries again, the helpers of the requests and the multipart bodies) without changing the behaviour; the absolute value of the most negative length no longer overflows (it saturates like the other operations).

## [0.0.8] - Adaptive layer height, elephant foot compensation, volumetric flow limit, time breakdown and the Bambu link seam with a simulated printer

- A print can use several materials. The module `multi` of the slicer takes the tools (a spool of a feeder or a head of a toolchanger, each with its filament, temperature and colour), the layers at which the tool changes and a purge tower: a wall on every layer and, where the tool changes, as many lines inside it as the purge needs. The purge grows with the difference of temperature and, going from a dark colour to a light one, with the difference of brightness, and a pair can be given its own figure. A tower that is not inside the bed, that comes near the part or that is too small for the purge, a tool that is too hot for the printer, two materials too far apart in temperature, a filament of another diameter and a change to the tool already in use are refused, and the plan is left as it was.
- The G-code of a print with several materials sets and waits for the temperature of the new tool and then picks it (`T0` to `T15`) before the purge, in the four dialects; the time of each change is in the estimate; the report says what each tool prints and what the purge and the tower cost.
- The figures of a print with several materials (the place and size of the tower, the purge, the time of a change, the most changes, the greatest step of temperature, and whether an optional kit is fitted) are keys of a settings file read by `MultiConfig::from_text`, each checked against its range with the line of a mistake.
- The catalog says how many tools a printer can use: `caps_of` reads the words of its multi-material system (a feeder of four, a feeder that grows to sixteen, a toolchanger of up to five, two heads) and tells a kit that may not be fitted, which the owner has to say is there before a print is planned for it.
- The top layer can be ironed (`ironing`, with `ironing_flow_percent` and `ironing_speed_mm_s`): one more pass over the solid skin, backwards, with very little plastic; a vase and a print with no top layers refuse it. The pressure advance (`pressure_advance`, 0 to 2) is written once before the first layer in the command of each firmware (`SET_PRESSURE_ADVANCE`, `M572` or `M900`) and the writer refuses a value outside its range.
- A Bambu Lab printer in LAN mode is made on the server through the client of the program with its certificate pinned (`MachineConnection::BambuLan`): nothing is sent without a fingerprint of 64 hexadecimal digits, the access code is written once and never printed (the other keys and codes are hidden from debug prints as well), and the three troubles of the link come back as `BambuProblem` from the stable codes `bambu_lan_off`, `printer_untrusted` and `printer_disconnected`, in eight languages. The switch `bambu_lan_enabled` of the server is read with its `ETag` and a `304` while it is the same, and the server in memory of the tests refuses as the real one does.
- The decision on the TLS of the Bambu link is written down: the program opens none, the server holds it, and a TLS of its own would need a crate behind a feature, with the list of tests that would close it.
- The window is laid out with the docking frame of the suite (`eh3d-dock`): every panel (the 3D view, the objects, the print settings, the materials, the layer preview, the G-code legend, the printer, the console, the device, the state of the machine, the queue and the project) can be taken away, put back, moved, tabbed, split, floated over the window, folded into an edge and maximised, and the arrangement can be locked. The four old tabs are now workspaces of that frame (Prepare, Review, Run and Setup, with Compact besides): choosing one is the same as choosing the tab, and the keys and buttons that went to a tab go to its workspace. A person can save an arrangement under a name.
- A menu bar above the window with File (New, Open, the list of recent files, Save, Save as, Export the G-code, Quit), Edit (the entries for what is done to the parts, with the ones for undo, cut, copy and paste turned off because the program has none), View and Window (made by the frame from the panels and the workspaces), Tools (slice, send to the queue, the views, the machines, the emergency stop) and Help (the online help, the list of shortcuts, a note about looking for updates, and About). The keys of the entries are the ones of the scheme of shortcuts of the program, so a key changed in the settings file is the key of its entry; the F1 of the frame no longer opens a second help.
- The arrangement of the panels, the workspaces, the shortcuts of the frame and the list of recent files are kept in the settings file in lines that start with `dock.`; they are written once when the arrangement changes (never at every frame, and never by a picture), a line that cannot be read gives back the factory arrangement of its workspace, and a settings file with no such lines gives the factory one. The module `panels` holds the panels, the workspaces, the menus, which entries are off at a given moment and the rule for saving as plain data with tests.
- The faults of the window that the pictures showed are fixed inside the new arrangement: the paths of the Project panel take the width of the panel and are written on their own line with their button; the queue has a panel of its own beside the state of the machine, so neither is narrow or half empty; a demonstration starts with the Bambu Lab P1S, the printer of the server made in memory, so the Prepare and the Run workspaces name the same machine; the demonstration goblet is built from smoothed rings so the shading has no bands; and the preview that looks inside draws the walls faint (`peek_wall_alpha`, from 0.05 to 1, 0.22 by default) so the infill is seen through them and not only at the mouth.
- Export the G-code of the plan to a file from the menu or from the Project panel (an existing file is not replaced unless it is the one this program wrote last); New takes the parts off the bed; Open and Save ask for the path where it is typed when none is typed; a file that was opened or saved goes to the list of recent files.
- The filaments and the resins come from the database of printing materials of the suite (`eh3d-printmaterials`) and no longer from tables of this repository: 477 filaments (the generic profiles and the ones makers publish, with the temperatures of the maker or, when it gives none, those of the generic profile of its polymer) and the seven generic resins with their exposure for a monochrome screen printer at the layer height they recommend. The twelve filaments and the seven resins the program had keep their old identifiers (`generic-pla`, `generic-tough`...) as aliases of the new ones (`generic/generic-pla`), with the values they had, so a settings file or a project that names one still finds it. The catalog is made once and copied after that. A filament profile file takes an optional `manufacturer` line and its identifier may have dots, plus signs, underscores and slashes.
- The filaments are looked for in the window by several words that meet in the name, the maker, the material and the identifier, and by maker and by material (all eighteen polymers of the database), with the number shown out of the total and only the rows in view drawn; a box says that the nozzle is a hardened one, so the filaments filled with fibre can be sliced, and the filter of the filaments that the printer can use follows the nozzle it is given.
- A check before printing in the console panel, from what the database says about the chosen filament: the temperatures against the range of the maker, the flow that the slice asks of the nozzle against the flow the filament takes, the drying the maker advises for a filament that takes water quickly, a hardened nozzle, a closed printer and a direct drive extruder; each line is a warning or advice, in the eight languages, and nothing is stopped by it.
- 24 new texts of the window in the eight languages (the panels, the menus, the export, the search of filaments, the check before printing), with the places for values checked in every language, and tests for the pure parts: the panels and their titles, the workspaces, the menu bar for both schemes of shortcuts (no entry without the key of the scheme, no key twice, none in conflict), what is turned off, when the arrangement is saved and that it reads back what was saved, the widths of the path fields, the smoothing of a profile, the faint walls, the settings that were added (`dock.*`, `peek_wall_alpha`), the new bed and the catalog read from the database.
- The continuous integration reads the common library of the suite at its release 0.2.7 (it was 0.2.6).
- The maturity of the project is declared as functional (it was scaffolding) in the manifest and in the badge and the status table of the eight READMEs; the evidence stays simulator.
- The Screenshots section of the eight READMEs shows the factory arrangement of each workspace (Prepare, Review, Run, Setup and Compact) with the menu bar, as drawings made from the model of the window by a new example (`cargo run --example workspace_pictures`, drawn with the picture maker of the docking frame, with no window and in any of the eight languages). The four photographs of the old tabs are taken out because they showed the window as it was before the docking frame. They are said to be drawings and not photographs; the photographs are taken again with the new manual workflow `Window pictures` (the picture mode on a virtual screen, kept as an artifact of the run), and it has not been run yet.
- **Requirements with identifiers and tests that name them:** `docs/REQUIREMENTS.md` is the register of what the program must do, one row for each capability with an identifier (`P3DP-001` on), its state (planned, prototype, implemented or validated), how far it is (done, partial, pending or blocked), its evidence and the test that shows it; a capability that is not written names the test that will show it. `tests/reqs.rs` reads the table and fails when a test does not exist, when a state does not agree with the progress or the evidence, when `docs/STATUS.md` does not say the same numbers, when a README does not link the documents or when a document holds a date or a private reference. The five documents of the family (`docs/SCOPE.md`, `docs/REQUIREMENTS.md`, `docs/STATUS.md`, `docs/NOT_YET.md` and `AGENTS.md`) are in the repository and are linked from the eight READMEs.
- **The READMEs say what the program does today:** the honesty check, the state of the window (five workspaces in the docking frame, never drawn on a screen and not looked at by a person), the features, the known limits and the next steps now agree with each other and with the roadmap, the note on the releases is true, and the list of documents no longer links the same file twice.
- The height of the layers can follow the lean of the walls (`adaptive_layers`, with `adaptive_min_layer_mm`, `adaptive_max_layer_mm` and `adaptive_cusp_mm`): a dome that lies flat gets thin layers and a straight wall gets the thickest, so the step between two layers stays under the cusp that was asked for and a part takes fewer layers than with one height. The first layer keeps its own height, the last one is whatever is left, the heights add up to the height of the model, they are kept to a hundredth of a millimetre, and a vase and a thickest layer above 80% of the nozzle are refused.
- The first layer can be drawn in from the outline (`elephant_foot_mm`, 0 to 2 mm) against the flared foot that the first layer leaves; the other layers and a first layer that stands on a raft are not touched, a part that is too thin for it is told as a thin region, and a value that would eat the whole wall is refused.
- A limit of the plastic that the nozzle melts (`max_volumetric_flow_mm3_s`, 0 is no limit): a line that would ask for more in a second is slowed down, never thinned, and never to less than 1 mm/s, in the G-code, in the estimate of the time and in the preview, so a print at the speeds of the settings and a print held to the flow of a hot end are the same plan at two paces. A limit under 1 is refused.
- The flow that the maker gives for a filament holds the slice to it: choosing a filament sets the limit of the flow of the slice to the maker's figure unless the person already holds a lower one, the command line says so (`flow: held to ...`), and the check before printing then finds the flow in order because the peak flow that the slice asks is now never above the limit.
- The report of a slice ends with where the time goes, by group of lines (perimeters, infill, solid skins, bridges, supports, adhesion, and the travel and the rest) as shares that add up to a hundred, in the eight languages and in the report of the command line, of the window and of the preview; the lines of each group are counted at the speed that is written, and what the estimate has beyond them (the travel, the retractions, the changes and the acceleration) is the last share.
- The place where a TLS connection would be plugged in is written and tried (`p3dp-link::tls`): a `TlsConnector` that opens a channel and a `TlsChannel` that carries bytes and shows the certificate, a `PinnedChannel` that only `connect_pinned` can make after the certificate was compared with the pinned fingerprint (a channel that is refused is closed before a byte is written, a handshake that fails leaves nothing open, and a certificate that changes between two connections is refused), and a connector for a build with no TLS that refuses and says to reach the printer through the server. The connector over a TLS library is not written, so it needs no crate: it would be a small crate outside the workspace, and the offline cache of the continuous integration is not touched.
- A Bambu Lab printer in LAN mode is made in memory behind those traits (`tls::sim`), speaking the MQTT of the link: it checks the user and the access code, grants the subscription, answers the request for the whole state with a report and takes a pause, a resume and a stop, sends its answers a few bytes at a time and can show another certificate, break the handshake or refuse the connection, and it counts the bytes that reached it and the channels left open.
- The stand-alone Bambu driver (`BambuLanDriver`) reads the state of the printer over the pinned channel into the normalized state (the state of the print, the nozzle, the bed and the chamber with their targets, the file, the share done and the time left in seconds, and an error code of the printer as an alert in hexadecimal), makes one short connection for each call, tells a wrong access code as an authentication error, a printer that cannot be reached or whose handshake breaks as no connection, and keeps the access code out of everything it prints.
- The same driver pauses, resumes and stops a print by the controls of the link, and refuses everything else (the emergency stop, the start of a file, the temperatures, the fan and raw G-code, and the upload) without writing a byte; the report of a printer is read as the figures it gives, a figure that is missing or is not a number is left out, a share is kept between 0 and 100, and a report cut or damaged at every position (a fixed seed) is an error or a state and never a panic. Nothing of this has run on a real printer or opened a real TLS connection.
- The documents say what changed: the register has nine requirements more (`P3DP-104` to `P3DP-112`), the page of what is not done and the notes on the server say what the program of itself can and cannot do with a Bambu printer and what a real connector would still need, the roadmap and the scope say that the slicer has several materials, the ironing, the pressure advance, the layers that follow the shape, the foot and the limit of the flow, and the eight READMEs say the same in their languages.

## [0.0.5] - Thumbnails and print summaries of g-code files, resins, profile editing, slice reports and queue rules

- Thumbnails and figures of the files that slicers write. A binary G-code file lists its thumbnail blocks (picture format, size in pixels, bytes) and hands out the picture of one only when it is not packed and starts with the signature of the format it says (PNG, JPEG or QOI); its metadata gives a typed summary (printer, filament, filament used in millimetres, cubic centimetres and grams, layer height, nozzle, time) in which a figure the file does not state stays empty. A plain G-code file is read the same way from its comments: the thumbnails written as base64 between `thumbnail begin` and `thumbnail end` (the declared length is checked and so are the bytes against the picture format) and the `key = value` figures, looking only at the start and the end of the file. `project FILE --thumbnail OUT` takes the largest thumbnail out under a name that matches its format and never over an existing file without `--force`. How far the scan goes is set in the settings file (`limit_gcode_head_kib`, `limit_gcode_tail_kib`, `limit_gcode_thumbnails`).
- Resins as data: seven generic resins (standard, water-washable, tough, flexible, castable, clear and high-detail) with the layer height, the exposures, the lift, the wavelength, the density and the wash as starting points, all `verified = false`. They are checked (the bottom layers are exposed at least as long as the others, only a resin printer takes resin), they read and write as a profile file where a mistake names the key, and the resin a part takes (millilitres, grams and the cost with the waste of the settings) is worked out. `phoenix-3dp resins [--kind KIND] [--printer ID] [--part-ml ML]` lists them.
- Six more filaments as data (PLA+, then PLA, PETG, ASA and PA with carbon fibre and ABS with glass fibre), the fibre-filled ones marked abrasive so that a brass nozzle is refused; a filament profile reads and writes as a file like the printer profiles do.
- Editing a printer profile: `check-profile ID --set "key=value;key=value"` applies the edits to a copy and checks the result as a whole. The id, the vendor, the model and the technology cannot be edited, and a key that does not exist (or does not exist for a resin printer) is named. It tells each line that changed, makes the limits the owner's, clears `verified` and, when a limit is raised past the figure of the maker (the bed of a Bambu Lab A1 mini from 80 C to 100 C by hand), allows it and says so in the eight languages. `--save FILE` writes the result as a profile file to edit further.
- A report of a slice: `slice --report` and the Preview tab tell the material (metres, cubic centimetres and grams by the density of the chosen filament, with the cost at the price of the settings), the time, the layers that take less than a set time, the slowest layers and the travel without printing, in the eight languages. The Preview tab also says where the print stands after the layer the slider is on (time gone, time left, share done) and can draw the travel moves in a colour of their own. The prices, the number of layers listed and the short-layer time are keys of the settings file (`price_filament_per_kg`, `price_resin_per_litre`, `resin_waste_percent`, `report_slowest_layers`, `report_short_layer_s`).
- The queue of the server: a job whose name repeats one that still waits or runs on the machine, or a line that already holds the most jobs the settings allow, is refused before anything is sent (`queue_refuse_duplicates`, `queue_max_waiting`); the Device tab keeps these rules, and the jobs can be grouped by machine into what runs, what waits in its order and what is over.
- Fixed: a time written with a unit of more than one byte in the metadata of a binary G-code file (for example `5é`) made the reader panic; it is now simply not a time. A fuzz test with a fixed seed cuts and damages the comments of plain G-code at every position to keep it so.

- The protocol of the Bambu local link, as bytes and text and without a socket: `mqtt` builds and reads the packets of MQTT 3.1.1 that the link uses (CONNECT with a user and a password, SUBSCRIBE, PUBLISH at quality 0, the pings; quality 1 and 2 and wildcards in a published topic are refused, every length is checked), `ftps` builds the commands of an upload and reads the replies (a short list of verbs, no line break in an argument, and a PASV that points anywhere but the control address is refused) and `pin` holds SHA-256 and the comparison of the fingerprint of a certificate. The settings of the link (`LanSettings`) are refused without that fingerprint, and the whole exchange is tried against a broker made in memory. The connection itself is not opened (there is no TLS in the standard library); the ways to close that are on the list of what is not done.

- Control of a resin (mSLA) print layer by layer (`resin_control`): the exposure a resin needs from the working curve of a photopolymer (the depth it cures to grows with the logarithm of the dose, so a layer height gives a dose and the power of the light gives seconds), the exposure of every layer (the bottom layers, the fade, the normal ones), and a lift that goes slower and waits longer under a large section. Every value is checked against the limits of the printer and the resin (exposure, bottom exposure, lift, speeds, waiting, dose of a layer); a layer that goes past one is an error that names the layer and the setting, and nothing is cut down. The figures of a resin are the person's to measure: the defaults are round figures of the usual order of size. The command `project` takes `--light-power MW` on a package of resin layers to tell the doses and check the limits.

- The slicer can help the first layer to hold: a skirt (`skirt_loops`, `skirt_distance_mm`), a brim that touches the part (`brim_width_mm`) or a raft under it (`raft_layers`, `raft_margin_mm`, `raft_air_gap_mm`), chosen with `adhesion`. They are paths like the rest, so the preview shows them (with their own colour and a switch), the time counts them and the G-code writes them; a loop that would leave the bed or a raft that makes the print taller than the printer is refused with the reason, never cut down. The part keeps the plastic of its volume; the extras are counted apart.
- A layer that would take less than `min_layer_time_s` is slowed down so that it takes that long, and no line goes below `min_speed_mm_s` unless its own speed was lower already; the first layer and the first layer on a raft are left alone.
- Retraction can follow the travel (`retract_adaptive`): a short travel pulls the filament back less than a long one, between `retract_min_percent` of `retract_mm` and all of it at `retract_full_travel_mm`; what is pulled back is pushed again, so the plastic of the file is still the plastic of the plan.
- A vase (`vase_mode`): one wall round one outline, a solid base of `bottom_layers` and nothing inside or on top; a part with a hole or more than one outline above the base is refused with the layer and the number of outlines, and a vase with supports or on a raft is refused.
- The time with acceleration is checked against a least and a most: the plain time (every move at its top speed) is the least and the time if the head stopped at every joint is the most, and `time_check` says whether the estimate lies between them; property tests of both over blocks and over sliced shapes from a fixed seed, and of the minutes written in the file.

## [0.0.3] - Richer slicer, 120 printer profiles and generic filaments, bgcode and resin package readers and the model of the 3D viewer

- The README in the eight languages, the architecture, the roadmap, the list of what is not done, the manifest and the two pictures describe the new parts and no longer say that the slicer has no supports, bridges, seams or variable width; the tree of files lists the new crate.

The drawing with wgpu is behind the feature `gui` of that crate, off by default and not compiled in the tests.
- The texts of the window in the eight languages for the new panels and controls: filament, infill and its patterns, supports, seam, bridges, layers, time estimate, resin, G-code file, the lift on travel, the fan ramp, the variable width, the controls of the viewer (orbit, pan, zoom, selection box, range of layers, reset, fit to the bed) and the three sources of the limits of a profile, which the report of a printer now names.
- The catalog has 120 printer models (it had 46), 112 for filament and 8 for resin, with the volume, the nozzles, the dialect and the ways of connecting taken from the facts of the study of printers; every model says `verified = false`, and each says where its temperature limits come from (`limit_source`): the Bambu Lab A1 and P1S and the rest of the first 46 carry the figures the maker publishes, and the 70 added filament printers carry the conservative limits of the program (260 C, 100 C, no chamber), which refuse more than the printer could take until a profile file raises them.
- Generic filaments for PLA, PETG, ABS, ASA, TPU and PA with a range of temperature for the nozzle, the bed and the fan, the density, the smallest nozzle and whether they are abrasive, and a check of a filament against a printer and a nozzle: a printer that cannot reach the temperature, a bed that is too cold, a nozzle the printer does not list, one that is too small or not hard enough are reasons that refuse the pair, the temperatures to use never go past the limit of the printer, and an enclosure or a direct drive extruder is advised where it helps. The new command `filaments` lists them and checks them for a printer (`--printer`, `--nozzle`, `--hardened`), and `slice` takes `--filament` to set the temperatures, the fan, the filament and the nozzle from one.
- A read-only reader of the headers of binary G-code files (`bgcode`): the version, the kind of checksum, the list of blocks with their compression and sizes, whether each CRC-32 matches, and the plain-text metadata (printer, filament, the time the file states); it never unpacks the blocks of G-code. It follows the public description of the format and has been checked only against files the tests make by that description.
- A reader of resin (mSLA) files by their headers only (`resin`): the open packages of layers (`.sl1`, `.sl1s`) give the layer height, the exposure times, the material and the number of layers without decoding an image; the closed formats of resin printers (`.ctb`, `.cbddlp`, `.photon`, `.pwmx`, `.goo` and others) are refused with the reason, and nothing is guessed about them. An estimate of the time adds the light, the waiting and the lift of every layer, with the exposure going from the bottom value to the normal one over the layers of transition.
- The command `project` also takes a binary G-code file and a package of resin layers (`--bottom-layers`, `--lift` for the estimate), and the fuzz tests cut, change and fill with noise the seeds of both readers.
- The slicer fills with a grid or a gyroid as well as with lines (`infill_pattern`); the gyroid is followed on a grid of squares and its period is worked out from the density, so the plastic it lays is near that of lines of the same percentage.
- A skin that hangs over nothing is a bridge: it is laid across the gap from one holder to the other, printed slower (`bridge_speed_mm_s`) with its own flow (`bridge_flow_ratio`) and its own fan (`bridge_fan_percent`), and a bridge longer than `max_bridge_span_mm` is refused instead of printed.
- Plain supports (`supports = buildplate` or `everywhere`): columns of the part are sampled, an overhang steeper than `support_overhang_angle_deg` with no lower material in its cone gets crossed lines under it with a gap at the top and a clearance at the sides; an overhang that would have to stand on the part while the supports are limited to the bed, or a decision that needs more samples than `max_support_samples`, is refused.
- The seam of the perimeters can be put at the corner nearest to the head, at the rear or lined up with the seam of the layer below (`seam`), and the perimeters of a part too thin for them may be made narrower, up to `width_variation_percent`, before the part is called thin; every path now carries its width and the plastic follows it.
- Retraction may be followed by a lift of the head on the travels that retract (`z_hop_mm`, refused when the print and the lift do not fit the printer), the part fan may climb over several layers (`fan_ramp_layers`), and there are speeds for solid infill, bridges and supports; no speed may be above `max_speed_mm_s`.
- The estimate of the time uses the acceleration and the corner speed of the settings (`accel_mm_s2`, `corner_speed_mm_s`): a planner that limits the speed at each joint by the angle and by the length of the move, with the plain estimate kept beside it as the least a print can take.
- The offset of a loop drops the points that lie on a straight stretch, which used to leave a short whisker next to a corner of the perimeters of a cut face.
- The CI is built against EH3D-COMMON v0.2.5 in the three workflows that name the tag of a sibling.
- The client of the printer plugin sets the target of a heater (`hotend`, `bed` or `chamber`, checked here against the stricter limits before anything is sent, and again by the server) and reads the answers shaped like Moonraker's and OctoPrint's when the machine has them on; the server in memory gives the same refusals as the real one (`temperature_over_limit`, `bad_heater`, `not_supported`).
